Why did your Mac lose charge overnight?

The Sleep tab shows what happened while your Mac slept: charge lost, how long, and how many times it woke. Then it shows what's holding it awake right now, honestly, including when it can't be sure.

The last sleep, explained

Not just that it slept. What happened while it did.

System Settings tells you the Mac slept. It doesn't tell you what happened: how much charge you lost, how long it took, or how many times something woke it. The Sleep tab's headline answers exactly that for the last sleep, then keeps the same numbers, drain, duration, wake count, and power source, for the sleeps before it.

When the readings at each end disagree, WhatBattery won't guess. A Mac that reads battery at both sleep and wake but somehow gained charge can't actually be right, a battery doesn't charge itself, so the power source shows as unclear rather than a wrong answer stated with confidence.

  • Charge lost, duration, and wake count for the last sleep
  • Recent sleeps listed with drain, duration, wake count, and power source
  • Power source shown honestly, including when the readings don't agree
  • Built to answer "why did I lose charge overnight", not just "that it slept"
Wake reasons and what's awake now

Plain English when it can prove it. The raw string always underneath.

Wake reasons come out of macOS as its own internal strings: hardware paths and interrupt codes, not anything written for a person to read. WhatBattery translates the handful whose meaning is documented and unambiguous, so a reason containing “rtc/Maintenance” reads as “Scheduled maintenance”, and opening the lid, a USB-C device being plugged in, and a notification arriving each get a label of their own. It groups and counts them across recent sleeps, and the original string sits underneath every one, translated or not.

Below that, WhatBattery shows what's holding the Mac awake right now, by process, with how long each has held it, split into what's actually blocking idle sleep and what isn't, with a filter to switch between the two.

  • Wake reasons grouped and counted, most common first
  • Plain-English label only where the mapping is certain
  • The raw system string shown underneath every reason
  • Awake-holders split into blocking idle sleep and not, with a filter
The promise behind every number

It says when it doesn't know, instead of showing a confident zero.

Every reading on this tab is one WhatBattery will withhold rather than guess at. It would rather tell you it can't be sure than hand you a wrong answer that looks certain.

No power source it can't back up

If a Mac reads battery at both sleep and wake but the charge went up, that's not possible, a battery can't gain charge on its own. WhatBattery calls the power source unclear rather than naming one anyway.

Only the wake reasons it can prove

Wake reasons are macOS's own internal strings. WhatBattery translates only the ones whose meaning is documented and unambiguous, and always shows the original underneath. It doesn't guess at the rest.

A failed read says so

If reading what's holding the Mac awake fails, WhatBattery says the read failed. It never shows an empty list as “nothing is holding your Mac awake”, a claim about the machine that our reading can't actually support.
