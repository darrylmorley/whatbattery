Battery runway

A window, or an honest silence.

Once there is enough history, WhatBattery fits a trend through the Mac's recorded health and projects the window in which it may reach 80%, the point Apple uses as a service reference.

The harder part is knowing when not to answer. A battery's health reading is the fuel gauge's own estimate: it sits still for weeks, then steps when the gauge recalibrates. A few weeks of that can look like a steep decline and is not one. WhatBattery tests whether the trend is real before it will name anything, and says nothing when it is not.

  • No projection until the evidence supports one
  • A date range rather than false precision
  • Recent wear rate in plain language
  • Faster-than-trend warning
Cells and internals

What the gauge knows and never shows you.

Your battery's fuel gauge keeps per-cell readings, and macOS surfaces none of them. WhatBattery reads them straight out: each cell's voltage and learned capacity, how far apart they are drifting, and the extremes the pack has seen across its whole life, including before you installed anything.

Cell balance is the useful part. A pack that is genuinely failing usually shows one cell pulling away from its neighbours well before the headline health figure moves.

  • Per-cell voltage, capacity and resistance
  • Cell balance, called out in millivolts
  • When the gauge last recalibrated, which explains sudden health steps
  • Lifetime temperature, voltage and current extremes
