True battery health

See the health number macOS rounds away.

WhatBattery computes health from the battery's raw maximum and design capacities, shows it to one decimal, and puts cycle count and service condition beside it.

No false precision, no hidden rounding

A rounded 100% hides the first signs of wear.

System Settings presents a whole number, so anything from 99.5% up reads as 100%. Fine at a glance, useless for spotting a trend. WhatBattery works from the underlying mAh figures and keeps one decimal place.

It also shows cycle count, temperature, voltage, the current charge state, and the same Normal or Service Recommended condition macOS uses. Where macOS's own capacity percentage differs from the unrounded figure, both are shown rather than leaving you to wonder which is right.

  • Health computed from nominal capacity ÷ design capacity
  • Apple's own service condition, not a competing verdict
  • macOS's rounded figure shown alongside when the two differ

The honest boundary

It tells you what the battery reports and what cannot be known.

There is no dependable public signal that proves a battery is genuine. WhatBattery does not add a green “genuine” badge to incomplete evidence. It shows the service condition, capacities, health, cycles, and identifiers the system can actually support.

Matches macOS

The condition is read from the system, so a service warning agrees with System Settings.

Does not control charging

WhatBattery observes. It never enforces a charge limit or writes to the battery.

Keeps missing data missing

If the system refuses a reading, the app shows that it is unavailable instead of guessing.
