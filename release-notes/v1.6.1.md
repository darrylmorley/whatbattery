# WhatBattery 1.6.1

The iPhone and iPad tab now knows the difference between a device that has gone quiet, one that has been unplugged, and one that cannot be read at all. macOS 14 and later, Apple Silicon.

## A Wi-Fi device going quiet is not a disconnection

A paired iPhone sitting on your desk is not steadily visible to your Mac. Measured over ten minutes on a locked phone, it was reachable 43% of the time, in bursts averaging two seconds, with gaps that ran past a minute. Earlier versions treated every one of those gaps as the phone being gone, so the tab flickered between a live reading and the setup guide while nothing was happening.

The tab now holds the last reading through a gap and tells you how old it is. Rows that only describe this instant, like the charging state, disappear once the figures are stale. Charge, cycles and temperature stay, because they are still worth reading a minute later.

## Unplugging is instant

Pull the cable and the device disappears at once, rather than after a wait.

WhatBattery now listens for the same attach and detach notifications Finder uses, so it is told the moment a cable comes out instead of inferring it from a poll that found nothing. That removes up to five seconds of a card describing a device already in your bag. Wi-Fi drops are deliberately ignored, because those are the ordinary rhythm above, and reacting to them is what caused the flickering in the first place.

## A device that will not read now says why

A connected device that could not be read used to be simply absent, so there was no way to tell an unsupported device from a locked one from a bad cable.

Each one is now listed with the reason: it never accepted a connection, it answered but reported no battery, it reported a battery in a shape this version does not recognise, or it reported values that cannot be right. Each carries advice that fits the actual problem. They also show up alongside devices that read fine, rather than only when nothing worked at all, so a broken iPad next to a working iPhone is visible instead of missing.

## One device, listed once

A device that is plugged in and Wi-Fi paired at the same time is offered to your Mac twice. It was appearing twice in the picker. It is now read once, over the cable first, falling back to the network if the cable does not answer.

## Exports no longer invent a temperature

A battery reading that carried no temperature was being exported as 0.000°C, which reads as a freezing battery rather than a missing measurement. CSV now leaves the column empty and JSON omits it. The same unmeasured readings were also dragging down the average charging temperature and appearing as real points on the temperature chart. Both now skip them.

## Clearer about what it is waiting for

Waiting for two devices no longer describes them as though they were one. A cabled iPad and a Wi-Fi iPhone are no longer both reported as "unplugged" when only one of them was, and the time since the last reading names the device it belongs to.

Ages are readable too. A tab left open overnight used to report "last read 572 minutes ago".

## Smaller things

- An iPhone with nothing plugged into it no longer reports a 0 W power adapter.
- A light and dark app icon for macOS 26.

Read-only as always: WhatBattery never changes how your devices charge.
