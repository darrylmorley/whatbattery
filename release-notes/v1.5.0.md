# WhatBattery 1.5.0

Answers the question every battery app leaves you with: what happened while you were asleep. macOS 14 and later, Apple Silicon.

## New: the Sleep tab (Pro)

Your Mac was at 80% when you shut the lid and 58% when you opened it. Nothing on the machine will tell you why.

The Sleep tab does. For the last sleep and the ones before it: how much charge went, how long it lasted, how many times something woke it, and which power source it was on. Then the wake reasons, grouped and counted, so a repeat offender is obvious rather than buried. Then what is holding the Mac awake right now, by process, with how long each has held it.

On my own Mac that last part named a menu bar app that had been holding a sleep assertion for eleven hours. I had no idea.

Wake reasons arrive from macOS as its own internal strings, hardware paths and interrupt codes. WhatBattery translates the handful whose meaning is documented and unambiguous, and shows the original underneath every one. The rest stay raw, because a plausible guess dressed as a translation is worse than an ugly string.

## New: your charge history starts full (Pro)

The Lifetime Analyzer used to open on an empty chart and tell a new user to check back later. Weeks of that before it had anything to say.

macOS has been keeping a sleep and wake log the whole time, with the battery percentage stamped on it. WhatBattery now reads that on first launch, so the charge chart opens with days or weeks of real history already on it, drawn as a separate line from anything measured since. It has only charge percent to work with, so it never pretends to be a temperature or voltage reading.

## It says when it does not know

Worth saying plainly, because it is the part that took the longest.

A sleep that reads "battery" at both ends but gained charge did not run on battery. Something was plugged in and unplugged while you were not looking. WhatBattery calls that power source unclear rather than printing a number a battery cannot produce. A reading it cannot take says the read failed, never an empty list implying nothing is happening. And an assertion that only keeps the screen on is described as exactly that, not as keeping the whole Mac awake, because those are different things with different costs.

None of that makes the app look cleverer. It makes it right, which lasts longer.

## Also

- The main window is wider, to fit the new tab without crowding the others.
- Charging habits, charge sessions, and per-app power are unchanged, and the Sleep tab sits alongside them rather than replacing anything.

Read-only as always: WhatBattery never changes how your devices charge.
