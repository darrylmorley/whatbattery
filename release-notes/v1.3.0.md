# WhatBattery 1.3.0

Battery readings you can trust, and a lot more of them. macOS 14 and later, Apple Silicon.

## The runway now knows when to keep quiet

The replacement forecast could take a few weeks of readings and confidently name a date months away. On a nearly new battery that is not a small error, it is the difference between "your battery is fine" and "start saving".

The cause: a battery's health figure is the fuel gauge's own estimate, and it does not drift smoothly. It holds steady for weeks, then steps when the gauge relearns the pack. Fit a line through a few of those steps and you get a confident answer to a question the data cannot answer.

WhatBattery now tests whether a decline is real before saying anything about it, and gives a window rather than a single month when it does. If your history is too short or too noisy, the runway says so instead of guessing. Some Macs that previously showed a replacement date will now show nothing. That is the fix working.

## New: Cells and internals (Pro)

Your battery's gauge keeps per-cell readings that macOS never shows. WhatBattery now reads them out:

- Each cell's voltage, learned capacity and resistance, with the balance between them called out. A pack that is genuinely failing usually shows one cell drifting from its neighbours long before the health percentage moves.
- The cycle count at the gauge's last recalibration, which is the usual explanation for health appearing to drop overnight.
- Lifetime temperature, voltage and current extremes, covering the pack's whole life including before you installed the app.

## New: macOS's own figure, beside ours

WhatBattery's health percentage is unrounded and comes straight from the gauge. macOS shows a rounded, smoothed one, and the two often disagree by a point or two. Rather than leave you wondering which is wrong, the header now shows both whenever they differ.

## New: a word about charging habits

Sitting at 100% on the charger is what wears a battery that is not being cycled, and the app has been measuring that all along without ever mentioning it. If your Mac spends most of its plugged-in time full, it will now say so on the This Mac tab, with the option to be notified about it in future. Nothing is enabled without you asking.

## Also

- The iPhone / iPad tab names the device you are looking at, with its own icon, and shows both devices at once when two are connected. A device that reports no name of its own is no longer assumed to be an iPhone.
- AirPods estimates read as listening time throughout, and short estimates show minutes rather than rounding everything under an hour.

Read-only as always: WhatBattery never changes how your devices charge.
