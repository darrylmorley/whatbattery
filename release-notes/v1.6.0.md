# WhatBattery 1.6.0

Fixes battery temperature, which has been wrong on every Mac since the first release. macOS 14 and later, Apple Silicon.

## Your Mac's temperature was barely moving

Every Mac has been showing its battery at roughly 30°C almost no matter what the battery was doing.

macOS publishes the battery temperature in tenths of a Kelvin. Apple's driver converts it to something friendlier on iPhones and iPads, but skips that step on macOS and hands over the raw sensor value. WhatBattery read it as though the conversion had happened.

That is not an offset, it is a squashed scale, which is why it went unnoticed for so long: the numbers always looked reasonable. On my own logged history a real range of 13.25°C to 37.15°C had been displayed as 28.64°C to 31.03°C. A 24 degree span shown as 2.4. A cold battery read far too warm, a hot one too cool, and the readings in between looked stable because they had been flattened, not because the battery was.

It is fixed at the point the reading is taken, so every screen agrees. Your logged history is corrected too, the moment you open the app: the old numbers can be converted exactly, so there is no step in the chart where the fix landed.

The gauge's own compensated reading, in the Pro cells card, used to sit a few degrees away from the main one. There was a caption explaining that gap as sensor versus compensated. That explanation was wrong. The two now agree to a fraction of a degree, which is what they always should have done.

## Lifetime extremes: 454°C

A user reported his MacBook Air's battery had reached 454°C, averaging 245°C, which would have been news to the aluminium.

Those were tenths of a degree: 45.4°C and 24.5°C. The battery gauge records the extremes it has lived through, but it does not say which scale it used, and it is not the same on every Mac. Of the machines checked, most report whole degrees and a sizeable minority report tenths. WhatBattery printed whatever it was handed.

It now works the scale out from the numbers themselves and checks that answer against the battery's current temperature. When the two disagree, it shows nothing at all. A figure that might be ten times out is worse than a blank row.

## Connected iPhones and iPads get their names back

An iPhone X showed up as "iPhone10,6" while an iPhone 15 Pro Max sitting next to it was named properly.

A connected device only tells your Mac its model identifier, never a readable name, so something has to translate. WhatBattery was using a list I had typed out, and that list started at the iPhone XS. Anything older fell through it.

It now reads the list macOS itself ships, the same one Finder uses. Two and a half times the coverage, the same names you see in Finder, and it stays current with macOS rather than waiting for me to update it.

## When it cannot read the temperature

A battery that reports nothing usable now says so, instead of showing 0.0°C.

That sounds small. It is the same class of bug as the 454°C, but harder to spot: nobody questions a battery reading 0.0°C on a cold morning, and once it was logged it sat in your lifetime statistics as the coldest that battery had ever been, permanently. Now it is left out of the figures, and the temperature alert cannot fire on a reading that was never taken.

Read-only as always: WhatBattery never changes how your devices charge.
