# WhatBattery 1.6.0

Battery temperature is now accurate, and connected iPhones and iPads are named properly. macOS 14 and later, Apple Silicon.

## Temperature now tracks the battery

Your battery's temperature moves a lot more than earlier versions let on. Idle in a cool room it can sit in the teens. Under a sustained charge it climbs into the high thirties. WhatBattery now shows that range as it happens.

macOS publishes this value in tenths of a Kelvin, unconverted, unlike every other Apple platform. Read on the wrong scale the readings compress toward the middle, which is why earlier versions hovered near 30°C whatever the battery was doing. On my own logged history a real 13°C to 37°C range had been showing as 29°C to 31°C.

Your logged history is corrected the moment you open the app. The conversion is exact, so charts read continuously across the update rather than showing a step where the fix landed. The gauge's own compensated reading, in the Pro cells card, now agrees with the main one to a fraction of a degree.

## Lifetime extremes read correctly

The Pro cells card shows the hottest and coldest the battery has been across its whole life, straight from the gauge. One MacBook Air was reporting 454°C, averaging 245°C, which would have been news to the aluminium.

The gauge records those extremes without saying which scale it used, and it differs between Macs: most report whole degrees, a sizeable minority tenths. WhatBattery now works it out from the numbers and checks the answer against the battery's current temperature. Where the two disagree it shows nothing. A figure that might be ten times out is worth less than a blank row.

## Connected iPhones and iPads are named

An iPhone X was showing as "iPhone10,6". A connected device only tells your Mac its model identifier, so something has to translate, and WhatBattery was using a list that started at the iPhone XS.

It now reads the list macOS itself ships, the same one Finder uses. Two and a half times the coverage, the same names Finder shows, and new models get named as macOS learns them rather than waiting for a WhatBattery update.

## Says "Unknown" rather than guessing

A battery that reports no usable temperature now says so, instead of showing 0.0°C. A missing reading is also left out of your lifetime figures rather than counted as an all-time low, and the temperature alert can't fire on a reading that was never taken.

Read-only as always: WhatBattery never changes how your devices charge.
